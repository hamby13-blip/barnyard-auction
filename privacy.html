What this app is

Barnyard Auction helps fair boards, 4-H clubs, FFA chapters, and similar groups run a livestock or small-animal auction sale: lots, buyers, exhibitor payouts, and buyer payment tracking. Accounts belong to an organization (your fair or club). Clerks sign in to enter and update sale data.

Information we collect and store

Account and organization. When someone creates an account we store their name, email address, and a hashed password. We store the organization name and which users belong to it (owner or clerk). Invite links store the invitee’s email until the invite is used or expires.

Sale records. Your organization’s clerks enter sale data you choose to keep in the app, including:

  • Event settings (name, dates, fee percentages, payout deadlines).
  • Lots: lot numbers, exhibitor names, species, club or chapter, animal weights, sale prices, add-ons, and assigned buyers.
  • Buyers: bidder numbers, names, optional business names, optional phone numbers, and whether each buyer has paid (tracking only — the app does not process card or bank payments).
  • Payouts: amounts owed to exhibitors, pickup status, dates, and notes.

Exhibitor names are often those of minors participating in fair programs. The app does not let children sign up or use the service on their own.

Technical data. We use an HttpOnly session cookie (ba_session) so signed-in users stay signed in for about 30 days. Server logs may record request method, path, status, and duration (not the contents of your sale forms). We do not use third-party advertising or analytics trackers in the app.

What stays in your browser

The app may store data in local storage on your device so clerking can continue if Wi‑Fi drops. This can include a copy of your organization’s sales, a cached user/org profile (not your password), theme preference, and similar settings. Clearing site data in the browser removes this cache; the authoritative copy for synced data remains on our server until you delete the organization.

Who controls sale and personal information

Your organization decides what to enter, who may clerk, and how long to keep records. You are responsible for having a lawful basis to collect and enter exhibitor and buyer information (including minors’ names) for your sale — for example, fair rules, parental consent, or club policies. Integenium provides software; we do not run your sale or vet each entry.

Children and teens

Barnyard Auction is meant for adult organizers and clerks, not for children to use directly. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13 through their own accounts. If you believe a child provided information to us in error, contact [CONTACT EMAIL].

How we use information

  • Operate accounts, organizations, invites, and syncing sale data between devices.
  • Host and back up organization data on our infrastructure.
  • Protect the service (authentication, rate limits, troubleshooting).

We do not sell your sale data or account information.

Where data is stored and who can see it

Data is stored in a PostgreSQL database hosted with Railway (or equivalent cloud hosting we use to run the app). Users in your organization who sign in can see that organization’s sales according to their role. Our hosting and operations staff can access systems as needed to run and secure the service, not for marketing.

Your browser may load fonts from Google Fonts to display the interface; that provider may receive basic request data from your browser.

Email

We store email addresses for accounts and invites. The app does not send automated emails today; invite links are copied and shared manually inside your organization.

Retention and deletion

We keep organization and sale data until the organization owner deletes the organization in the app (or we delete it after a long period of inactivity if we adopt such a policy and notify you). Deleting the organization removes associated users, sales, invites, and sessions from our database. Owners should export backups before deletion if they need records later.

Security

Passwords are stored hashed. Sessions use signed tokens. We use HTTPS in production. No method of storage or transmission is 100% secure; use strong passwords and limit who has clerk access.
